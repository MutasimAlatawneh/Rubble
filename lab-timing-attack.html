Client-Side Timing Attack

Recover a secret one letter at a time, just by timing how long a wrong guess takes.

1 What it is

A timing attack learns a secret by measuring how long a program takes to answer. If a password check stops at the first wrong character, a guess with more correct letters takes a little longer, so an attacker can rebuild the secret one character at a time. It belongs to OWASP Top 10:2021 A02 – Cryptographic Failures and is tracked as CWE-208 (Observable Timing Discrepancy).

4 Why it matters

Guessing a 5-letter secret blindly can take up to 265 ≈ 11.9 million tries. The timing leak cuts that to at most 26 × 5 = 130. The same trick works against API tokens, password-reset codes and message signatures (HMACs), which turns “impossible to guess” into “a few minutes”.

Real incident: the Xbox 360 boot loader (2007) checked signatures with memcmp, which stops at the first wrong byte. Hackers timed it to forge a valid signature byte by byte and boot their own code. In 2009 the same bug was found in Google’s Keyczar crypto library.

5 The fix

Compare every character, no matter what, and collect the differences with XOR (^) and OR (|). The loop always does the same work, so time reveals nothing.

✅There is no early exit: a right guess and a wrong guess cost exactly the same five comparisons.

In real projects, use a vetted helper such as crypto.timingSafeEqual (Node.js) or hmac.compare_digest (Python).
